# Medicare Part D drug spend: drivers, savings and 2026 outlook

**To:** VP of Pharmacy / GPO pharmacy program team
**From:** Sudamshu Rao
**Data:** CMS Medicare Part D Spending by Drug (2020 to 2024) and CMS NADAC. All figures are gross drug cost, before manufacturer rebates.

## Summary

Part D drug spend grew 49% in four years, to $288.7B in 2024, and almost none of that growth came from higher prices. It came from more prescriptions of existing drugs and from new drugs, concentrated in a short list of cardiometabolic brands. Savings programs should therefore focus on which drugs are used and which version is dispensed, not only on unit price. Three actions follow: concentrate contracting and utilization management on the roughly 40 drugs that make up half of spend, run an adalimumab biosimilar conversion program, and budget the 2026 Medicare negotiated prices on a net basis, because the gross figure overstates the savings about sixfold.

## Findings

### 1. Growth came from volume and new drugs, and price netted to zero

| Driver, 2020 to 2024 | Change in gross spend |
|---|---|
| More units of existing drugs | +$65.1B |
| Drugs launched since 2020 | +$31.3B |
| Price changes within existing drugs | −$1.9B |
| **Total** | **+$94.6B** |

The price line is a net figure. Brand-name drugs added $15.0B through price increases, while generic-named drugs (−$9.0B) and insulin (−$7.8B, after the 2024 list-price cuts) took it back.

By class, GLP-1 drugs (+$20.7B) and SGLT2 inhibitors (+$13.9B) account for over a third of all growth, and both grew mainly through volume. Ozempic alone added $11.5B, all of it from more units; its price per unit fell.

Spend is concentrated: **38 of 3,625 drugs make up half of 2024 spend**, 220 make up 80%, and the top 10 make up 29%.

### 2. Adalimumab biosimilars cost far less and are barely used

| 2024 | Gross spend per unit |
|---|---|
| Humira | $3,506 |
| Biosimilars, all (blended) | $2,045 |
| Biosimilars with a low list price | $638 |

Biosimilars had **4.6% of adalimumab units** in 2024, while Humira still carried $5.7B of gross spend. Biosimilars launched at two list-price levels: one close to Humira's and one far below it.

| Share of Humira units switched | Gross savings, blended price | Gross savings, low-list price |
|---|---|---|
| 25% | $0.6B | $1.2B |
| 50% | $1.2B | $2.3B |
| 75% | $1.8B | $3.5B |

These are gross figures. Humira carries large rebates, so the net savings depend on contract terms that are not public.

### 3. The 2026 negotiated prices look large on paper and smaller after rebates

Ten Part D drugs have Medicare negotiated prices from January 1, 2026, at 38% to 79% below their 2023 list prices. Several are among the largest drugs by spend: Eliquis (#1), Jardiance (#3) and Xarelto (#5).

| | Estimate |
|---|---|
| 2024 gross spend on the ten drugs | $61.3B |
| Estimated gross reduction at negotiated prices | $37.5B (61%) |
| CMS estimate, net of rebates, if the prices had applied in 2023 | about $6B (22%) |

The gap between the two estimates is the rebates already being paid on these drugs. Gross spend sits close to list price, so a gross calculation counts discounts that payers were already receiving.

## Outlook

A straight-line trend, chosen over two alternatives because it had the lowest back-test error (4.3% on average, 5.2% at worst), gives:

| | Gross spend | Range |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 forecast | $317.8B | $301.3B to $334.3B |
| 2026 forecast | $343.0B | $325.1B to $360.8B |
| 2026 with negotiated prices | $295.5B | $280.2B to $310.9B |

The range is the largest back-test error. With five yearly data points, a tighter range would claim precision the data does not support.

## Recommendations

1. **Focus on the short list.** Put contracting, formulary and utilization management effort on the roughly 40 drugs that make up half of spend, starting with GLP-1s, SGLT2 inhibitors and anticoagulants. Because their growth is volume-driven, utilization programs (prior authorization criteria, step therapy, adherence and appropriate-use reviews) will move spend more than price negotiation alone.
2. **Run an adalimumab biosimilar conversion program.** Prioritize low-list-price biosimilars, and confirm the net economics against current Humira rebate terms before switching. A 50% conversion is worth $1.2B to $2.3B gross a year at Part D scale.
3. **Budget 2026 on net, not gross.** Use the net figure (about 22% on the ten drugs) for 2026 budgets, and re-baseline rebate expectations for these drugs, since rebates on them will shrink as negotiated prices take effect.

## Limitations

- **Gross, not net.** Manufacturer rebates are not public, so all spend and savings figures are gross.
- **Part D only.** Clinic-administered drugs (Part B) are out of scope.
- **Earlier years slightly understated.** The current CMS release appears to drop drugs no longer dispensed. Its 2021 total ($213.5B) is about 1% below the $216B KFF reported from an earlier release, so 2020 to 2024 growth is slightly overstated.
- **Price per unit includes mix.** Price is gross spend per CMS dosage unit within each drug, so a shift between strengths of the same drug shows up as a price change.
- **No public benchmark for specialty drugs.** $53.9B of the analyzed spend (23%) is specialty drugs and vaccines that NADAC does not survey. For this spend, a GPO's own contract and purchasing data is the only benchmark.
- **Drug classes are rule-based.** Classes come from ingredient-name rules for the classes that drive spend; 36% of 2024 spend stays in "Other".

## Next steps

- Add Part B (clinic-administered) drugs, where most oncology infusion spend sits.
- Add the second round of negotiated prices (effective 2027), which includes the semaglutide products.
- Replace name-based drug classes with a standard classification (for example, USP categories) via an RxNorm crosswalk.
